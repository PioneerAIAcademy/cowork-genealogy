# Locality Guide: Brooklyn, Kings County, New York (1880–1900)

## Jurisdiction overview
- **Formed:** Kings County established 1683 (from Dutch colonial jurisdiction). Brooklyn incorporated as a city 1834; it remained an independent city until 1 January 1898, when it was consolidated into New York City as the Borough of Brooklyn.
- **County seat:** Brooklyn (also the city itself)
- **Parent jurisdiction:** New York State
- **Boundary change:** On 1 January 1898, Brooklyn ceased to be an independent city and became a borough of New York City. Records before 1898 were filed with City of Brooklyn or Kings County; records after 1898 were filed with New York City. FamilySearch standardPlace shifts from "Brooklyn, Kings, New York, United States" (pre-1898) to "Brooklyn, New York City, New York, United States" (post-1898).
- **Ethnic composition (1880s):** Large German, Irish, and Scandinavian immigrant communities; growing Italian and Eastern European presence by late 1880s–1890s.

## Boundary changes
- Kings County boundaries were stable throughout the 1880–1900 period.
- **Critical administrative change (1 January 1898):** Brooklyn consolidated into New York City. Pre-1898 vital records were filed with the City of Brooklyn Registrar; post-1898 records with the NYC Department of Health. Both periods' records are now held primarily at NYC Municipal Archives.
- When searching FamilySearch, use "Brooklyn, Kings, New York, United States" for 1887 events and "Brooklyn, New York City, New York, United States" for post-1898 events.

## Available record types

### Vital records (civil registration)
- **Start date:** New York City (including Brooklyn) began civil registration of births in 1847. Registration compliance improved significantly through the 1870s–1880s.
- **What exists:** Births (1847–1909 on FamilySearch), marriages (1829–1938 on FamilySearch), deaths (1846–1909 on FamilySearch)
- **Primary FamilySearch collections:**
  - **New York, New York City Births, 1846–1909** (collection 2240282) — 2,795,266 indexed records, no images on FamilySearch. Covers Frederick's 31 July 1887 birth. **Indexed + searchable by name.**
  - **New York, New York City Marriage Records, 1829–1938** (collection 2143225) — 2,942,326 records, 155,452 images. Parents' marriage record could name their parents. **Indexed + images.**
  - **New York, New York City Municipal Deaths, 1795–1949** (collection 2240477) — 6,209,923 records (date range 1846–1909 for this scope), no images. **Indexed.**
- **Also on Ancestry:** "New York, New York, U.S., Extracted Birth Index, 1878–1909" — directly covers 1887 Brooklyn births. "New York, New York, U.S., Extracted Death Index, 1862–1948."
- **Original records:** Held at **NYC Municipal Archives** (31 Chambers St., New York, NY). Certified copies of post-1910 records available from NY State Department of Health (health.ny.gov/vital_records/).
- **Gaps:** Pre-1847 civil registration did not exist; earlier births rely on church records. Some 1880s registrations were delayed or omitted due to incomplete compliance.

## Records NOT online
- Original birth certificate images for many Brooklyn 1880s births are not yet digitized on FamilySearch (collection 2240282 has no images — index only). Full-image access requires NYC Municipal Archives.
- Many church records remain undigitized at individual parishes or diocesan archives.
- Brooklyn City Directories (valuable for locating the Munson family's address and occupation) are not on FamilySearch but available at Ancestry and Brooklyn Public Library.

## Known record losses
- **1890 Federal Census:** Largely destroyed by fire in 1921. The 1890 NYC Police Census (FS collection 2381996) and the 1892 New York State Census (FS collection 1529100) are the primary substitutes for Brooklyn households in this period.
- No major courthouse fires are recorded for Kings County during this period; probate and deed records are generally intact.

## Research tips
- **Prioritize the birth certificate:** The 31 July 1887 Brooklyn birth record should be searched first in FamilySearch collection 2240282. It typically names both parents, their ages, birthplaces, and occupations. If found, this may answer the research question directly.
- **Use the 1892 NY State Census and 1890 Police Census** as substitutes for the destroyed 1890 federal census. Frederick (born 1887) would be age 4–5 in 1892 and appears with parents.
- **Search under both "Brooklyn, Kings, New York" and "New York City"** when searching FamilySearch, as the consolidated city's records post-1898 use a different standardPlace.
- **Parents' marriage record** (FS collection 2143225) often names parents of both spouses and gives precise marriage date/place — a strong secondary source even if the birth record is found.
- **Naturalization records for the father** (if immigrant) can name his birthplace, arrival date, and sometimes spouse and children.
- **Brooklyn Daily Eagle** digitized at Brooklyn Public Library and Chronicling America — birth/marriage/death notices were commonly published.
- **Steve Morse's NYC tools** (stevemorse.org/nyc/faq.htm) provide supplementary search interfaces for NYC vital records.
