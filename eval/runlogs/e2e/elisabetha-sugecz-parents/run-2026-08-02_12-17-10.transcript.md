# Locality Guide: Madžarevo (Magyarlaka), Varasd, Croatia-Slavonia (ca. 1750–1900)

## Jurisdiction overview
- **Historical name:** Magyarlaka (Hungarian); Madžarevo (Croatian); village in Varasd County, Kingdom of Croatia-Slavonia
- **Modern equivalent:** Madžarevo, Varaždin County, Croatia (46.17°N, 16.31°E)
- **County seat:** Varaždin (Varasd)
- **Parent jurisdiction:** Habsburg Monarchy → Kingdom of Croatia-Slavonia under Austria-Hungary
- **Predominant religion:** Roman Catholic (~97% of Varasd County was Croatian/Catholic per 1910 census)
- **Record language:** Latin primarily; Croatian and Hungarian also appear

## Boundary changes
- **1102–1526:** Kingdom of Croatia in personal union with Hungary
- **1526–1871:** Habsburg Monarchy; much of Varasd County under the Varaždin Generalate (Military Frontier)
- **1871–1918:** Autonomous county (Varasd) within Kingdom of Croatia-Slavonia, Austro-Hungarian Empire
- **1918–1922:** Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes
- **1992–present:** Republic of Croatia

## Status animarum (soul registers)
Two large browse-only volumes cover 1659–1910 (`005498001_001/002`). These household census-type registers list all parishioners by family, making them invaluable for reconstructing household composition and relationships even when individual event records are unclear.

## Civil registration
Civil registration began **May 1946** under Yugoslavia — not relevant for 18th–19th century research.

## What marriage records reveal (per Croatia Church Records wiki)
Croatian Catholic marriage registers typically record: date and place; names of bride and groom; civil statuses; places of origin and residence; **names of parents**; and names of witnesses. — This is why the marriage record is the primary source for Elisabetha's parentage.

## Physical repositories
- **Croatian State Archives** (Hrvatski Državni arhiv), Marulićev trg 21, 10000 Zagreb — registers >100 years old transferred in 1957; [ARHiNET](http://arhinet.arhiv.hr/index.aspx) has a growing inventory of digitized vital registers.
- **Varaždin State Archives** — holds material microfilmed by FamilySearch; local church records.

## Research tips
1. **Search indexed volumes first.** The directly-indexed Magyarlaka volumes (100% recordSearchablePercent) are the fastest path — search via FamilySearch record search with collection 1743180 (Hungary Catholic Church Records) filtered to Magyarlaka/Varasd.
2. **Marriage gap 1798–1848.** If the marriage isn't in the indexed volumes, browse the unindexed image volumes or search Hungary, Church Books 1624–1950 (coll. 4133831), which may include entries not in the Magyarlaka-specific index.
3. **Status animarum as a supplement.** The soul registers (1659–1910) can corroborate family groupings and reveal parents even when a baptismal entry is elusive — they list whole households.
4. **Latin throughout.** Records in this parish are almost entirely Latin through the 19th century. Key terms: *natus/a* (born), *baptizatus/a* (baptized), *copulati* or *matrimonio juncti* (married), *pater* (father), *mater* (mother), *patrini* (godparents), *testes* (witnesses).
5. **ARHiNET** (arhinet.arhiv.hr) is a Croatian digital archive that complements FamilySearch, especially for records not yet digitized to FS.
