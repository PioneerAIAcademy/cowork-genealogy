# Locality Guide: Madžarevo (Magyarlaka), Varasd County, Croatia-Slavonia (18th century, 1700–1800)

## Jurisdiction overview
- **18th-century name:** Magyarlaka, Varasd, Croatia-Slavonia, Hungary
- **Modern name:** Madžarevo, Varaždin County, Republic of Croatia
- **County seat:** Varaždin (Varasd)
- **Political belonging (target period):** Part of the Habsburg Monarchy, 1526–1871. Varasd County lay within the Kingdom of Croatia-Slavonia under the Habsburgs. In the 18th century, much of Varasd County was under the Varaždin Generalate of the Military Frontier until that was dissolved in 1871.
- **Predominant religion:** Roman Catholic (overwhelmingly, 97.7% Croatian by 1910)
- **Language of records:** Latin (18th-century parish registers); Croatian and Hungarian also appear in later periods
- **Diocesan jurisdiction:** Diocese of Varaždin (Archdiocese of Zagreb umbrella)

## Boundary changes
- **1102–1526:** Part of the Kingdom of Croatia in personal union with Hungary
- **1526–1871:** Habsburg Monarchy — records filed under Hungary for FamilySearch collection scoping
- **1720:** Lost northern Međimurje region to Hungary's Zala County (temporarily regained 1850) — does not affect Madžarevo
- **1871–1918:** Re-organized as an autonomous county within the Kingdom of Croatia-Slavonia, Austro-Hungarian Empire
- **1918–present:** Part of Yugoslavia, then Republic of Croatia (1991)
- **Research implication:** FamilySearch organizes collections at the country level outside the US/Canada/Mexico, so Varasd County records appear under **Hungary** scope in `collections_search`. When searching the FS catalog, look for this village under both "Madžarevo" (Croatian) and "Magyarlaka" (Hungarian), as both names appear in records and finding aids.

## Available record types

### Church records — PRIMARY SOURCE for 18th-century parentage research
- **Denominations present:** Roman Catholic (primary); FamilySearch also holds Orthodox, Greek Catholic, Evangelical, Reformed Christian volumes for Croatia broadly, but this parish is Roman Catholic
- **Record types:** Baptisms (Rođeni), Marriages (Vjenčani), Burials (Umrli), Status animarum (soul registers)
- **Language:** Latin
- **What marriage records contain:** Date and place of event, names of bride and groom, civil status, places of origin, **names of parents**, names of witnesses — this is the key field for parentage research
- **What baptism records contain:** Date and place of baptism, infant's name, gender, legitimacy, **parents' names**, residence, place of origin, godparents

### Vital records (civil registration)
- **Start date:** Civil registration in Croatia did not begin until **May 1946** (introduced throughout Yugoslavia). No civil vital records exist for the 18th century.
- **Pre-registration:** Church registers are the only source class for 18th-century vital events.

### Census records
- **No national census records** for this region in the 18th century as we understand them today
- **Status animarum** (soul registers) serve as the equivalent of census/household enumeration for Roman Catholic parishes — the Magyarlaka soul register spans 1659–1910 and covers the full target period (browse-only, two volumes totaling 427 images)
- Later Habsburg-era censuses (mid-19th century) fall outside the target window

### Land and probate records
- County-level land and probate records were held at Varaždin County administrative offices
- **Varaždin State Archives** (Državni arhiv u Varaždinu) holds these — FamilySearch microfilmed material from this archive but not all has been digitized online

### Military records
- The Varaždin Generalate of the Military Frontier operated in this region during much of the 18th century; military service records may exist but are specialized and not likely to name parents directly
- Not a primary avenue for parentage research in this case

### Cemetery records
- BillionGraves and FindAGrave have Croatia indexes, but coverage of 18th-century Varasd County is limited — burial entries in the parish Umrli (death) registers are the primary source
