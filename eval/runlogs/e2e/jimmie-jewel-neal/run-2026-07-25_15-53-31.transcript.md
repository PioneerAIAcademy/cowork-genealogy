# Locality Guide: Blount County, Alabama (1840–1870)

## Jurisdiction overview
- **Formed:** 1818 from the Creek Cession; Blount County is one of Alabama's original counties
- **County seat:** Oneonta (courthouse at Blountsville during the antebellum period)
- **Parent jurisdiction:** Alabama (statehood 1819)
- **Population during period:** Population data service unavailable; Blount was a rural, predominantly small-farm county in north-central Alabama
- **Economy:** Subsistence and mixed farming; very few large slaveholders (a "yeoman county"); small amounts of cotton
- **Ethnic composition:** Predominantly Anglo-American settlers of Scots-Irish and English origin from Georgia, the Carolinas, and Virginia
- **Religious denominations:** Baptist (dominant), Methodist, and some Presbyterian congregations — "Free State of Winston" sentiment in the north of the county
- **Key historical events:** Civil War strongly divided — many Unionists and deserters; county contributed men to both Confederate and Union forces; the Free State of Winston (adjacent Winston County) ideology influenced northern Blount

## Boundary changes
- Boundaries were largely stable 1840–1870. Winston County was carved from Walker County (not Blount) in 1850, so no territory was lost. Cullman County was formed from portions of Blount, Morgan, Winston, and Walker in 1877 — after the target period.

## Available record types

### Vital records (civil registration)
- **Start date:** Alabama did not require statewide civil birth or death registration until 1908
- **What exists for 1840–1870:** None — no civil birth or death certificates for this period
- **Marriage records:** Marriage records begin with county formation (1820). FamilySearch has indexed volumes covering 1820–1871 (see Census/Marriage section below)
- **Pre-registration alternatives:** Church records, probate records naming heirs, census mortality schedules (1850, 1860, 1870)

## Known record losses
- No major courthouse fire reported for Blount County (unlike many Alabama counties). Records are generally intact.
- Some records damaged in the Civil War period due to disruption and movement

6. **BLM Land Patents**: Search M. M. Wood for a federal land patent in Blount County — would fix his arrival date.
7. **Full-text search deed records**: Deed volumes are full-text searchable (not name-indexed) — use FS full-text search on the deed volumes for "Wood."
8. **Blount County was Unionist**: Search both Confederate AND Union military records for male Wood relatives.
9. **Free State of Winston Bible Records**: The freestateofwinston.org site covers Blount, Winston, and Walker Counties; check for Wood family Bible records.
