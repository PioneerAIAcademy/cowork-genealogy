# Person Evidence Rubric

Grading dimensions for person-evidence unit tests. Evaluated by the LLM judge alongside the base rubric (correctness, completeness).

## Confidence calibration

Is the confidence level (confident/probable/speculative) appropriate for the IDENTITY evidence strength — how certain we are that this record's role IS the tree person? A single census co-residence is "probable" at best — "confident" requires independent corroboration of identity. Informant reliability (information_quality, informant_proximity) is a quality of the source classification, not a basis for identity confidence: a single primary-informant source with no corroborating record is still `probable` on this scale, not `confident`. A chronological gap between the record's birth/christening date and the tree person's known birth year that cannot describe the same birth event on its face (e.g. an Irish Catholic baptism follows birth within days, so a 13-year gap is a presumptive contradiction, not date noise) caps the link at `speculative` unless the record itself explains the delay (conditional baptism, adult baptism, catch-up registration). Without such an explanation, `confident` on a contradicted link is a fail and `probable` is a partial.

- **pass:** Confidence tier matches identity-evidence strength: `confident` when multiple independent sources corroborate the identity link; `probable` when a single source supports the link with no contradicting evidence; `speculative` when only circumstantial alignment or when a chronological or qualitative contradiction exists.
- **partial:** Confidence is off by one tier — `confident` claimed on a single source with no corroboration, or `speculative` chosen when solid corroboration exists, or `probable` retained despite a clear chronological contradiction.
- **fail:** Confidence claim is clearly inverted (e.g., `confident` on a single source with no corroboration; `speculative` on a fully corroborated link; `confident` on a record whose date contradicts the tree person's birth by more than a few years).

## Rationale quality

Does the rationale explain why this record's role is believed to be this person? It should cite specific matching attributes (name, age, location, family context), not just "names match."

- **pass:** `rationale` cites multiple specific matching attributes (e.g., "name matches; age consistent with subject's ~1845 birth; located in Schuylkill County where subject is known to have lived; no other Patrick Flynn of matching age in the county") and addresses why competing candidates were ruled out.
- **partial:** Rationale cites attributes but is missing the disambiguation step ("name and age match" without noting whether other candidates exist).
- **fail:** Rationale is generic ("names match") or asserts identity without inspecting alternatives.

## Multi-person awareness

When an assertion implies a relationship (e.g., "listed as son of Thomas"), did the skill create person_evidence links for both persons? The assertion bears on both the child and the parent.

- **pass:** For every relationship-implying assertion, both person_evidence entries exist — one for each person in the relationship. In a review/audit request (where no writes should be made), correctly flagging that a relationship assertion is missing its other-side link — and asking before creating it — satisfies this dimension.
- **partial:** Most relationship assertions get both links, but one or two have only one side linked.
- **fail:** Relationship assertions consistently only link to one side (typically the subject), missing the implied other-person link.

## Person minting and connecting edges

When an assertion's persona (or a household member the record introduces) matches no existing GedcomX person, did person-evidence mint it itself via `materialize_facts` — the persona form (create-or-enrich, carrying the record's SOURCED facts) when that party has its own persona on the record, or the named-party form (a SOURCED name, and no facts, because the assertion supports none) when the record only names her — link the assertion(s), and write the connecting parent-child / spouse EDGE(s) via `tree_edit` `add_relationship` with a source-ref? person-evidence owns the household skeleton; record-extraction is assertion-only. (Only graded when the scenario has an unmatched persona or household members absent from the tree; mark N/A otherwise.)

- **pass:** Mints each new person via `materialize_facts` so it arrives with a resolved non-null source-ref on every name and fact it writes, and links the assertion(s). Two shapes are both correct: the persona form arrives WITH its sourced facts (at minimum the sourced name, plus `gender`); the named-party form arrives with a sourced name and, where the record gives that party their own persona corroborating their role, may also carry sourced facts from that persona — writing additional facts in that case is also a PASS. `gender: "Unknown"` is correct where the record asserts no sex. Any parent-child / spouse edge the record establishes is written via `tree_edit` `add_relationship` carrying a source-ref resolved from the relationship assertion's `source_id`: a directly-stated relationship (a will's "my son") is direct evidence; a pre-1880 census relationship (inferred from household position, no relationship column) is INDIRECT, materialized at lower ref quality. For a multi-person household, the `merge_warnings` dry-run coherence gate is handled correctly: run over the pre-materialization set before committing **when a candidate record document is in hand**, and otherwise skipped with the reason noted in the affected rationale — a scenario built from pre-extracted assertions supplies no candidate document, and `record_read` is not this skill's to call, so a noted skip is the pass there, not a shortfall. An existing tree person unexplainedly absent from the record is FLAGGED as an identity question — never renamed or overwritten. Confidence is calibrated to the evidence (a single source → `probable`, not `confident`); `match_score` is null for a full-text-sourced assertion.
- **partial:** Mints and links but leaves a member a name-only shell in the OLD stub shape (a `tree_edit add_person` name with no source-ref; a materialize_facts named-party mint is not this), omits a record-established edge or writes one without a source-ref, mis-classifies a pre-1880 inferred edge as directly-stated, skips the `merge_warnings` gate on a household **while holding a candidate record document**, or skips it without noting why, is malformed (missing gender/name), over-claims confidence, or hedges by deferring the mint to a later step.
- **fail:** Forces an assertion onto a non-matching person, skips the role leaving the persona unlinked, does not build the household skeleton, writes edges with no source provenance, or overwrites/renames an existing tree person to force a record match.

## Score discipline

Is `same_person` actually invoked when a serious tree candidate exists for the assertion — and once invoked, is the score treated as an input that informs, never replaces, the correlation analysis? (Only graded when a tree candidate is in play; mark N/A otherwise.)

**How the assertion was retrieved does not decide whether it can be scored.** `same_person`'s project-relative form takes `{ projectPath, assertionId, treePersonId }` and resolves the record side itself — the retained sidecar, a fresh read, or, failing both, a persona projected from the record's own extracted assertions (issue #1731). So a full-text hit, an image-, external-site- or PDF-sourced assertion and a sidecar-less search are all scorable, and a null `record_persona_id` says nothing about scorability. Two cases legitimately have no score, and the tool names both back to the caller: the record holds no persona for the party this link is about, and the tree person was minted from the very persona being scored (circular — it can only confirm itself).

- **pass:** `same_person` is called whenever a tree candidate is in play, whatever the assertion's provenance; each link is scored against the right record party. **`recordRole` is OMITTED for the assertion's own party — omission means exactly that, and supplying it there is redundant, not better.** It is required only for the SECOND party of a relationship or marriage assertion, which gets its own call; reusing the first party's persona for that second link is the error; the score is recorded in `match_score` and informs — not dictates — confidence (a high score does not auto-link past a qualitative conflict; a low score on a thin subject does not dismiss a strong qualitative match). Where the tool reports no persona for that party, or the candidate is a stub minted from this very persona, correlation stands alone and `match_score` is legitimately null with the reason stated.
- **partial:** Uses the score correctly in direction but leans on it more than the correlation warrants — e.g., lets a high score nudge confidence up despite a noted conflict, or hesitates on a strong variant match because of the low score, without actually inverting the decision.
- **fail:** `same_person` is never called despite a tree candidate (a mandatory score silently skipped) — including skipping it because `record_persona_id` is null, or because the assertion came from a full-text hit, an image or a sidecar-less search, none of which are reasons any more; or the first party's persona is reused for a second party's link, manufacturing a near-zero score; or a called score overrides correlation — a `confident` link past a qualitative conflict because the score is high, or a strong qualitative match dismissed because the score is low, or `match_score` treated as the verdict.
