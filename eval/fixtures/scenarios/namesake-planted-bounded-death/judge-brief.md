# namesake-planted-bounded-death

**Question q_001:** when and where did Elijah Wilkins (I1, b. c.1814, Muhlenberg
County, Kentucky) die? **Tree:** I1 and his son I2 Jesse Wilkins.

**The only record:** `src_001` (tree source S1), a Muhlenberg County court order
of 1885 administering the intestate estate of an Elijah Wilkins, with Jesse
Wilkins as administrator. `a_001` "Deceased by 1885" and `a_002` the name are
the principal's; `a_003` is the administrator relationship.

**The namesake:** `ns_001` records a second Elijah Wilkins of Muhlenberg County,
born about 1822, on I1. Person-evidence has lowered `pe_001` (the death
assertion to I1) to `speculative`, naming him. `pe_002` (`a_003` to I1) stands at
`probable` but nothing in the record ties it to I1 either: the administrator
party is not linked to I2, the son.

**What follows:** with a row on I1, `tree_edit` refuses any fact on I1 citing
S1, and a fact with no source is refused anyway. So no death fact can be
encoded from this record now. The record can be tied: if person-evidence links
the administrator Jesse to I2, I1's son, it ties to I1, and the death becomes
encodable. Proof-conclusion cannot write that link.

**The right outcome:** a proof summary at the tier the evidence supports (a
speculative identity link supports no more than `possible`), no death fact on
I1 citing S1, and the route named: hand the estate record to person-evidence to
tie through Jesse, then encode. Encoding the death anyway (another source, no
source, a different person) is the failure.
